# Types of Hormones Used in MTF HRT

MTF hormone therapy typically involves two main categories of medications: estrogens to develop feminine characteristics, and anti-androgens to suppress masculine characteristics.

## Estrogens

Estrogen is the primary feminizing hormone. It's responsible for developing feminine secondary sex characteristics.

### Types of Estrogen

#### Estradiol (Most Common)

**Oral Estradiol**
- Brand names: Estrace, Gynodiol
- Taken as pills, typically 1-3 times daily
- Dosage: Usually 2-8 mg per day
- Pros: Convenient, adjustable dosing
- Cons: First-pass liver metabolism, may be less efficient

**Transdermal Estradiol (Patches)**
- Brand names: Climara, Vivelle-Dot, Estraderm
- Applied to skin, changed 1-2 times weekly
- Dosage: 0.025-0.2 mg per day (delivered dose)
- Pros: Steady hormone levels, bypasses liver, lower clot risk
- Cons: May cause skin irritation, visible

**Estradiol Gel**
- Brand names: EstroGel, Elestrin
- Applied to skin daily
- Dosage: 0.75-3 mg per day
- Pros: Flexible dosing, bypasses liver
- Cons: Must avoid skin-to-skin contact until dry, variable absorption

**Injectable Estradiol**
- Estradiol valerate or cypionate
- Injected intramuscularly every 1-2 weeks
- Dosage: 5-30 mg per week (split into 1-2 injections)
- Pros: Effective, no daily routine, good hormone levels
- Cons: Requires injections, hormone levels fluctuate

**Sublingual Estradiol**
- Same medication as oral, but dissolved under tongue
- Taken 2-4 times daily
- Dosage: 2-8 mg per day
- Pros: Better absorption than oral, bypasses first-pass metabolism
- Cons: Multiple daily doses, short half-life

### Synthetic Estrogens (Less Common)

**Ethinyl Estradiol**
- Found in birth control pills
- Generally NOT recommended for transgender HRT due to higher clot risk
- More potent than estradiol but with more risks

## Anti-Androgens

Anti-androgens suppress testosterone production or block its effects.

### Types of Anti-Androgens

#### Spironolactone (Most Common in US)

- Brand name: Aldactone
- Dosage: 50-300 mg per day
- Mechanism: Blocks androgen receptors and mildly suppresses testosterone production
- Pros: Well-studied, relatively safe, inexpensive
- Cons: Diuretic effects (increased urination), may cause dehydration, potassium retention, not very potent

#### Cyproterone Acetate (Common in Europe/Canada)

- Brand name: Androcur
- Dosage: 12.5-50 mg per day
- Mechanism: Strong anti-androgen and progestin, suppresses testosterone production
- Pros: Very effective at suppressing testosterone
- Cons: Not available in the US, risk of meningioma (rare brain tumor) at high doses, liver toxicity risk, fatigue, depression

#### GnRH Agonists (Very Effective but Expensive)

- Examples: Lupron (leuprolide), Zoladex (goserelin)
- Dosage: Injectable or implant, typically monthly or every few months
- Mechanism: Suppresses production of sex hormones from the testes
- Pros: Very effective, no daily pills
- Cons: Very expensive, requires injections, may cause bone density loss

#### Bicalutamide (Emerging Option)

- Brand name: Casodex
- Dosage: 25-50 mg per day
- Mechanism: Blocks androgen receptors
- Pros: Well-tolerated, effective, once-daily
- Cons: Expensive, off-label use, potential liver risks, less long-term data

#### 5-Alpha Reductase Inhibitors

- Examples: Finasteride (Propecia), Dutasteride (Avodart)
- Dosage: Finasteride 1-5 mg/day, Dutasteride 0.5 mg/day
- Mechanism: Blocks conversion of testosterone to DHT (more potent androgen)
- Pros: Helpful for hair retention, minimal side effects
- Cons: Only blocks DHT, not a complete anti-androgen, used as adjunct therapy

## Progesterone (Optional)

The role of progesterone in MTF HRT is debated.

### Bioidentical Progesterone

- Oral or rectal administration
- Dosage: 100-200 mg per day
- Potential benefits: May enhance breast development, mood effects, libido
- Considerations: Limited evidence for benefits in transfeminine HRT, potential risks not well-studied

### Synthetic Progestins

- Generally not recommended
- Higher risk profile than bioidentical progesterone

## Orchiectomy Considerations

After surgical removal of the testes (orchiectomy):

- Anti-androgens are typically no longer needed
- Estrogen doses can often be reduced
- Simplified medication regimen
- Reduced costs

## Choosing the Right Regimen

The best hormone regimen depends on:

- Individual health factors
- Access to medications
- Cost and insurance coverage
- Preference for administration method
- Response to treatment
- Healthcare provider expertise

Work with your healthcare provider to find the regimen that works best for you. Regular monitoring ensures safety and efficacy.
